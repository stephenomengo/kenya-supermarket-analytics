# Kenyan Supermarket Sales & Customer Analytics

End-to-end data analytics project by **Stephen Omengo Owuor** (LuxDevHQ Data Science & Analytics capstone): from generating and cleaning data, through SQL, statistics and forecasting, to a Power BI dashboard.

> **Data notice:** all sales, store, product and customer data is synthetic (generated by `src/data_generation/generate_data.py`). The only real data is the monthly inflation series from the Central Bank of Kenya.

## The problem
A Kenyan supermarket chain with 10 stores in 10 counties wants to know what drives its sales and how to plan stock, staff and promotions better. The project answers seven questions (full text in `docs/problem_statement.md`):

1. How do sales vary by month, weekday, payday and holiday, and by store?
2. Which categories and products drive revenue and margin?
3. Did promotions increase sales enough to justify the discounts?
4. Do M-Pesa customers spend more than cash customers?
5. Which customers are the most valuable?
6. Does inflation relate to basket size?
7. Can weekly demand be forecast better than a simple baseline?

## Key findings
Data: 24 months (Jul 2023 to Jun 2025), 302,076 cleaned sales lines, 112,202 baskets, **KES 78.9M revenue**, 21.9% gross margin, average basket KES 703.

- **Calendar drives sales:** weekends +25%, paydays +28%, public holidays +36%, December about +33%. Revenue was flat year on year.
- **Concentrated stores and products:** six urban stores make 73% of revenue (Westlands alone 16%). The top 10 of 98 products make 48%.
- **Customers:** 42% of revenue comes from guests with no customer ID. 24.5% of identified customers ("Champions") produce 66.5% of identified revenue.
- **Promotions did not pay:** no extra units sold, and net revenue per product-day was 14% lower on promotion days (discounts cost KES 645k).
- **No effect found** for payment method, loyalty tier, location type or inflation (tested with assumptions checked and Holm correction).
- **Forecast:** a Ridge model predicts weekly revenue per store with an error (MAE) of KES 8,130 (12.0%), 31% better than the best baseline chosen on validation. The unavoidable noise is about 11%.

**Recommendations:** staff and stock for weekends, paydays and December; protect the top stores and products; push loyalty sign-up at the till and redesign the tiers; stop or redesign the discount promotions; use the weekly forecast for planning.

## How to reproduce
**Requirements:** Python 3.10 or newer (Anaconda works well), packages in `requirements.txt`; PostgreSQL 16 or newer for the SQL step; Power BI Desktop for the dashboard.

```bash
# 1. Install the environment (pick one)
pip install -r requirements.txt
#   or: conda env create -f environment.yml && conda activate kenya-retail

# 2. Rebuild data, notebooks and tables in order (about 5 minutes)
bash run_all.sh
#   if "python" is not the right interpreter:  PYTHON=/path/to/python bash run_all.sh
```

`run_all.sh` runs, in this order:

| Step | What | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `src/data_generation/generate_data.py` | `data/raw/*.csv` |
| 2 | `01_cleaning/01_cleaning.ipynb` | `data/clean/sales_clean.csv`, `cleaning_log.csv` |
| 3 | `02_eda/02_eda.ipynb` | `02_eda/figures/` |
| 4 | `statistical_analysis/statistical_analysis.ipynb` | `test_summary.csv`, figures |
| 5 | `04_forecasting/forecasting.ipynb` | `model_comparison.csv`, `test_predictions.csv`, figures |
| 6 | `05_dashboard/prepare_dashboard_data.py` | dashboard tables in `05_dashboard/data/` |

**SQL (milestone 4)** needs PostgreSQL, so it is run separately from the project root:
```bash
createdb -U postgres kenya_retail
psql -U postgres -d kenya_retail -f 03_sql_analysis/schema.sql
psql -U postgres -d kenya_retail -f 03_sql_analysis/load_data.sql
bash 03_sql_analysis/run_queries.sh postgres kenya_retail
```

**Dashboard (milestone 7):** open `05_dashboard/kenya_retail_dashboard.pbix` in Power BI Desktop. See `05_dashboard/README.md`.

## Known limitations
- **Simulated data.** Calendar effects and store sizes were built into the generator. Payment method, loyalty tier and promotions have no built-in effect, so "no effect found" describes this dataset, not real retail.
- **Customer signup dates** are not tied to purchases: 17.6% of identified purchase lines are dated before the customer's signup date. Cohorts therefore use first purchase month.
- **Only 24 months of data**, so the forecast has seen each seasonal event once or twice, and the inflation test has low power (n = 24).
- **Dashboard data source.** The Power BI file was built from the raw sales table (302,680 lines), not the cleaned one (302,076). Its revenue card therefore shows about KES 79.04M instead of 78.89M, and quantities include the duplicate and negative lines removed in milestone 2. All other milestones use the cleaned data.
